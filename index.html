Common Standards
01 Common Standards

Without a shared rubric, every conversation about AI readiness is a conversation about different things. Common standards make comparison possible—and comparison is what makes growth visible and collective rather than private and accidental.

Meaningful Evidence
02 Meaningful Evidence

Most assessments measure what people reveal under observation. CIRA reads what people actually do—the conversation record they already have, assessed by the AI that was there. The evidence is honest because it was never performed for an audience.

Shared Language
03 Shared Language

A team that cannot name a gap cannot close it together. When everyone has the same vocabulary for what good co-intelligence looks like, feedback stops being personal and starts being navigable—a specific, shared, actionable map.

Every Voice Reaches
04 Every Voice Reaches

Common standards remove status and fluency as filters on who gets heard. The team's best thinking comes from its best thinkers—not its loudest ones. When every voice reaches, alignment becomes a byproduct of the work rather than a managed outcome.

Built-In Buy-In
05 Built-In Buy-In

New ideas don't need a rollout when the reasoning already circulated. Co-intelligent teams arrive at alignment through shared inquiry rather than managed persuasion. There is nothing to roll out because there was never a gap to bridge.

Durable Insight
06 Durable Insight

Knowledge discovered through shared inquiry forms schemas—reasoning patterns, not just answers. When someone leaves, the way the team asks questions stays. That is what makes co-intelligent institutional memory different from documentation.
